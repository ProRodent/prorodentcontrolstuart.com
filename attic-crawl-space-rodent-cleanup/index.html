What rodents leave in an attic.

An attic with rodents is more than droppings. Rats and mice leave urine, trails of grease, shredded insulation, nests of paper and fabric, and sometimes chewed ductwork. Over a season or more, that material soaks into insulation and rafters, and the smell can find its way into the living space.

In Stuart, attics also house the air handler and duct runs for many homes. Rodents nest near warm equipment, chew duct wrap and leave droppings on and around the system. That makes cleanup a matter of comfort, health and efficiency as much as tidiness.

Why the heat matters here.

South Florida attics get very hot, and humidity does not stay out. Heat speeds the breakdown of droppings and urine into fine dust and strong odor. Moisture helps mold grow on contaminated insulation. A cold-weather attic can hold the same mess for years with little smell, but ours tends to announce itself.

Because dried droppings turn to dust, sweeping or vacuuming them can put particles in the air. The CDC advises against it. Instead, droppings and nests are dampened with disinfectant and removed while wearing protective equipment.

What cleanup involves.

  • Protective equipment for the technician, including respirator and gloves.
  • Dampening droppings and nesting material with disinfectant before removal.
  • Bagging and disposing of contaminated insulation and debris.
  • Spot or full sanitizing of surfaces, rafters and duct areas.
  • Replacing removed insulation where the homeowner chooses.
  • Odor treatment where urine has soaked in.

Not every attic needs full removal. Light contamination can be handled by spot cleaning and sanitizing. Heavy contamination, wet insulation or a dead animal in the insulation may call for full removal and replacement. We look at the space, explain which category it falls in and price it before starting.

Cleanup comes after trapping and sealing, not before.

Crawl spaces and raised foundations.

Not every Stuart house has an attic with a hatch. Some older homes have crawl spaces under raised wood floors, and some have small attic voids over additions. These tight spaces need similar care, with added attention to moisture, vapor barriers and screened vents.

In older neighborhoods near downtown, wood-frame homes may have crawl spaces open at the vents. Rats and mice use them to move in and out. We check that the vents are screened and that the space is dry enough that cleanup will last.

Timing and the order of work.

The right order is to remove the animals, seal the entry points, then clean. Cleaning first only invites a new group into fresh material. We handle this through rodent trapping and removal and exclusion and sealing before scheduling the cleanup.

After cleanup, replacement insulation, if needed, goes in the same visit when possible. Replacing insulation also restores the energy performance of the attic, which matters when cooling runs most of the year.

When cleanup is not enough.

Sometimes the attic problem is bigger than droppings. Wet insulation from a roof leak, mold on rafters or a strong ammonia smell that survives cleaning all point to a moisture issue. Rodents are often a symptom of that, since they favor damp, warm, quiet places.

In those cases we describe what we see and recommend the right trade, such as a roofer or a mold specialist, rather than layering cleanup on a wet attic. Cleaning a space that keeps getting wet only buys a few months.

What cleanup does for the house.

Cleanup is not only about appearance. Removing soiled insulation lowers odor, restores insulation value and removes the scent trails that draw new animals. It also lets us inspect the framing and ducts, which are hidden under the old material.

After the work, you get a plain summary of what was removed and what was replaced, with photographs where we can take them.

What you can do before we arrive.

Stay out of the attic and keep children and pets away. Do not sweep. Take photos of anything you can see from the hatch, such as droppings, nests or chewed cable. If you notice a strong smell, tell us, since it can mean an animal has died in the space and our dead rodent removal service applies.

Wiring chewed through is a hazard. Do not touch it. Mention it on the call so the plan includes an electrician or a repair referral.

Tell us on the call

  • Whether you can see insulation damage from the hatch.
  • Any strong odor in rooms below the attic.
  • Whether the air handler is in the attic.
  • Whether you have a crawl space or raised foundation.

Questions about attic and crawl space rodent cleanup

Do I need to replace all my attic insulation?

Not always. Light contamination can be cleaned in place. Heavy contamination or wet insulation usually needs removal and replacement.

Is cleanup done before or after removal?

After. We trap and seal first so the cleaned space stays clean.

Can I do it myself?

Small spot cleanups are possible with proper protection, but large attics are best left to professionals with respirators and disposal methods. Follow CDC cleanup guidance if you do.
