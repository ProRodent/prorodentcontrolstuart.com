Palm City, described honestly.

Palm City is an unincorporated community, a census-designated place in Martin County, governed by the Board of County Commissioners rather than by a town council of its own. It carries ZIP code 34990 and sits about 4.0 miles from Stuart, across the St. Lucie River to the west. Because it shares a county with Stuart, the local rules that apply to a property are county rules.

Being unincorporated changes who you call about permits and roads. It does not change how we inspect a house. The same inspect, trap and seal order applies to a Palm City address as to one inside Stuart.

What shapes rodent pressure here.

A community near the river tends to have more vegetation and more water frontage than an inland subdivision. Palms, hedges and mature trees give roof rats overhead routes, while canals, ponds and landscaping beds give Norway rats cover at ground level. We ask about the lot, because a house on a wooded or waterside parcel is a different job than one in the middle of a clear lot.

Housing varies from older properties to newer developments, so entry points vary by street. Some houses have worn vents and aging soffits. Others have gaps at tile roof returns and unsealed line sets that appeared when the trades finished.

What people in Palm City call about.

A short list of reasons accounts for most calls from a community like this one. Matching mouse versus rat from a phone description is unreliable, so we confirm in person.

  • Scratching or movement over a bedroom or hallway ceiling at night.
  • Droppings found in a garage, laundry room or pantry.
  • A burrow or worn path along a fence or beside a shed.
  • Gnawed fruit under a tree, or chewed pet food bags.
  • A musty or sharp smell in a closed room or attic.
A short drive across the river does not change how carefully we inspect.

How treatment and sealing work.

After the walk-through identifies what is active and where, the plan follows two parts. Rodent trapping and removal clears what is inside, then rodent exclusion and entry point sealing closes the gaps with metal and mesh so the problem does not reopen.

For lots with palms, fruit trees or a waterside edge, backyard and outdoor rodent control often comes up as part of the same visit. Trimming limbs from the roof and picking up fallen fruit reduce what draws the animals in.

What to expect from the first visit.

The technician starts outside and works in. The roofline, the vents, the garage door and the base of the walls come first, then the attic, garage and any room where you noticed signs. You are walked through what was found, and the plan and the price come before any work.

If the problem is minor, we say so. If it is larger, such as a roof rat colony in an attic with damaged insulation, we explain the order of work and what each step costs. You decide what to do, and nothing starts until you agree.

Afterward, we return to check the traps and confirm that the seals are holding. Activity that continues means a gap remains, and we widen the inspection.
