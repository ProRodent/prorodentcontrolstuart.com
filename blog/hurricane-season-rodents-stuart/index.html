Storms flood burrows, tear roofs and fill garages with boxes. All three help rodents find their way into the house.

Hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30, with the peak usually from mid August through October. Every Florida homeowner knows the drill of shutters, supplies and generators. Fewer people think about rodents, yet storms change their behavior in ways that matter. Water, wind and clutter all play a part.

This guide explains how a storm season affects rodents around Stuart, and what to check before and after a storm. None of it is dramatic, but a few small steps can prevent a pest problem from layering on top of storm cleanup.

What storms do to rodent habitats.

Heavy rain floods burrows. A Norway rat burrow beside a slab or under a shed can fill with water, and the animals leave to find dry ground. The nearest dry ground is often a house, a garage or a shed. After a big rain, calls about rats and mice often rise within days.

Wind tears things loose. A lifted tile edge, a torn soffit or a loose vent cover creates a new opening that was not there before. A fallen tree changes the route to the roof. Debris piles near the house give animals new places to nest.

Power outages affect food. Spoiled food, especially from a refrigerator or freezer, draws animals, and the cleanup bags put at the curb can attract them too. Compost and fruit that fall in a storm add to the supply.

Before the storm: the house.

Preparation tasks overlap with pest prevention. If you are checking the roof, shutters and openings, add a few rodent checks. Look at the roofline for tile that is loose or has gaps at the ends. Check that gable vents and soffit panels are intact, with metal mesh behind any vent.

Garages deserve attention. Preparation brings supplies into the garage, such as water, batteries, canned food and pet food. Store these in sealed containers and keep them off the floor. Cardboard boxes of supplies are nesting material, so use plastic bins where possible.

  • Check tile ends, ridge caps and soffits for gaps or loose pieces.
  • Confirm gable and soffit vents have intact metal mesh.
  • Store supplies and pet food in sealed plastic or metal containers.
  • Check the garage door seal and the line between door and slab.
  • Trim branches that touch the roof before the storm, not after.
  • Take out trash and remove fallen fruit from the yard.

Before the storm: the yard.

Trimming before a storm helps with wind and with rodents. Branches that touch the roof become bridges, and a storm can shift them closer. Palms with dead fronds drop debris, and clean crowns hold fewer nests.

Move stored items off the ground and away from the foundation. Firewood, lumber, tarps and boxes against the house give animals shelter and a short path to the wall. If you must stack them, keep them a foot or two from the wall and off the ground.

Pool equipment, sheds and outbuildings also need a look. A shed with a gap beneath the door can host a nest. If you are fitting shutters or securing items, spend a minute checking the base of these structures.

After the storm: the first walk-around.

When it is safe to go outside, walk the property. Look at the roofline for new gaps and at the base of walls for new holes, burrows or displaced vent covers. Check that garage doors still seal to the slab, since a warped door leaves a gap.

Inside, check the attic from the hatch if the roof took damage. Water stains, displaced insulation or new daylight are all signs of a breach. A hole that lets in rain lets in rodents too. Photograph anything you find, since that record helps with insurance and with the pest visit.

Clean up carefully. If you find droppings or nesting material during storm cleanup, do not sweep or vacuum dry material. Wear gloves and a mask, wet it with disinfectant and bag it, following CDC guidance.

Temporary and permanent repairs.

A tarp is not a rodent barrier. If a roof opening was covered temporarily, the edges and the gaps may still allow entry. Once the roofer has finished, ask for a check of tile closure strips and flashing, and have us inspect the roofline for gaps that remain.

Permanent sealing uses stainless mesh, flashing and sealant that rodents cannot chew. That work is described on our exclusion and entry point sealing page. We recommend scheduling it before peak season where possible, or soon after any storm damage is repaired.

If you already hear noise or see droppings, rodent trapping and removal comes first. Sealing before trapping can lock animals inside, and that creates odor problems later.

A one-page preparation list.

  • Roofline checked for tile gaps and loose soffits.
  • Gable and soffit vents screened with metal mesh.
  • Supplies stored in sealed bins, off the floor.
  • Branches trimmed back from the roof before the storm.
  • Fruit, trash and pet food cleared from the yard.
  • Photographs taken of the exterior for your records.

Tape the list inside a cabinet door, and review it each June. It takes minutes and covers both storm readiness and rodent prevention.

Evacuations and empty houses.

If you leave for a storm or for the season, the house sits quiet. Rodents notice quiet. Before you go, empty and clean the refrigerator if power may fail, take food out of the pantry or seal it in tight containers, and remove trash. Leave nothing edible on counters.

When you return, walk the house before settling in. Look for droppings in cabinets and the garage, listen for noise in the attic and check the vehicles. Our vehicle and car rodent removal page covers engine bay nests in cars left idle.

What to do, and not do, while you wait

These steps follow CDC cleanup guidance for rodent droppings and nests. They are general safety practice for any household.

  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests. That can put contaminated dust in the air you breathe.
  • Ventilate a shut-up space first. Half an hour of airing out is the CDC's starting point.
  • Gloves on first, handwashing last. Do both every time.
  • Soak the droppings and the surface in disinfectant first. Wiping comes only after the soak time.
  • Bag the waste, seal the bag, and put it in the trash. Do not handle live or dead rodents with bare hands.
  • Any fever, aches or breathing trouble after handling rodent waste should be checked by a doctor. We do not make health claims about individual exposure.

Sources and standards

Common questions

Do hurricanes really increase rodent calls?

Heavy rain and storm damage can push animals toward buildings and open new entry points, so calls often rise afterward.

Should I seal before or after a storm?

Seal before peak season when you can, and recheck after any storm. If animals are already inside, trap first and seal second.

Is a tarp enough to keep rodents out?

No. A tarp is a temporary cover for rain. Edges and gaps still need proper sealing once repairs are done.
