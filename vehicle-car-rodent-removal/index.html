Why rodents choose vehicles.

A parked car looks like a safe shelter to a rat or mouse. The engine bay is warm after a drive, sheltered from the weather and full of soft material. Air filters, insulation and cabin filters make good nests. A vehicle that sits for weeks, such as a seasonal car, a boat on a trailer or an RV in storage, is quiet and undisturbed.

Stuart has many vehicles that sit for long periods. Owners who spend part of the year elsewhere leave cars in garages or driveways. Boat owners park trailers on the side of the house. Each is a target when food and cover are close by.

Signs of rodents in a vehicle.

The first sign is often a smell, a musty or urine odor from the vents when the fan starts. Others are warning lights from chewed wiring, droppings on the engine cover or under the hood, shredded material in the air filter box and gnaw marks on hoses. Some owners find stored food wrappers or nuts tucked into corners.

  • Musty or urine smell when the fan or AC starts.
  • Dashboard warning lights or sensors that stop working.
  • Droppings on the engine cover, battery tray or under the seats.
  • Shredded air filter or cabin filter material.
  • Gnawing on wiring insulation, hoses and plastic covers.
  • Stored food, seeds or nesting material in corners.

Rodents chew wiring because their incisors never stop growing and they need to wear them down. Some vehicle wiring insulation uses soy-based materials, which some drivers believe attracts chewing. Whatever the cause, a chewed harness can cost a lot to repair.

What we do.

We inspect the engine bay, air intake, cabin air filter and trunk, plus the space around the parked vehicle. Nests and droppings are removed using protective gear, and surfaces are sanitized. Traps or stations are placed in the garage or parking area to catch any animals that remain.

We do not repair vehicles. If wiring is chewed, we document the damage with photos and suggest you take the car to a mechanic. That record can also help with an insurance claim where applicable.

The nest in the car is a symptom. The real question is where the animal lives.

Finding the source.

A rodent in the engine bay almost always lives nearby. We look at the garage, carport and yard for the colony, and we check where the vehicle is parked. A car under a tree, beside a hedge or near a fruit tree is easy to reach. We then handle the colony through rodent trapping and removal and exclusion if the garage is the entry.

Prevention starts with the parking spot. Park away from hedges and palms if possible. Keep the garage clear of cardboard and stored food. Start and move a seldom-used car regularly, since motion and noise discourage nesting.

Storing a vehicle in Florida.

If you will be away, take simple steps. Remove food and wrappers from the cabin and trunk. Clean the car so no crumbs remain. Park in a sealed garage if you have one, with a tight door seal. Open the hood slightly if it is safe to do so, which removes the dark sheltered feeling rodents prefer.

Boats and trailers need a similar look, with attention to storage compartments, cushions and covers. Cushions and canvas are tempting nest material. Check them after a long idle period.

What to do if the car will not start.

If a warning light appears or the car will not start after a long idle period, do not keep trying. A chewed harness can short when power is applied, and repeated attempts can damage more components. Disconnect the battery if you can do so safely and call a mechanic.

When the car is back in order, we can look at where it was parked and what drew the animal. Without that step, a repaired harness can be chewed again within weeks.

Golf carts, lawn equipment and small engines.

Vehicles are not the only machines that rodents like. Golf carts in a garage, riding mowers in a shed, generators and pressure washers all have wiring, filters and warm parts. A generator that fails to start before a storm is a poor surprise.

We include stored equipment on a vehicle visit, and we suggest simple steps to keep it clean, such as covering air intakes and storing items off the floor.

Before we arrive

  • Close off the room with the most activity if you can, and keep doors shut behind you.
  • Avoid stirring up dust in attics or closets before we arrive.
  • Do not poke at nests or burrows, which can scatter the animals into new areas.
  • Photograph droppings, gnawing or damage with your phone so we can see what you saw.

Questions about vehicle and car rodent removal

Do you repair chewed wiring?

No. We document the damage and clear the animals. A mechanic or auto electrician handles the repair.

Can I clean the nest myself?

Wear gloves and a mask, dampen droppings rather than sweeping and bag the material. Cabin air filters often need replacement.

Do you work on boats and RVs?

Yes, at your address. We inspect compartments and storage areas and place traps nearby.
