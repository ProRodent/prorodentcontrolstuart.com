What rodents damage.

Rodent incisors grow constantly, so gnawing is a habit, not a choice. They chew wood, plastic, wiring insulation, foam, drywall and soft metals. The damage ranges from cosmetic to dangerous. A gnawed corner of a pantry door is a nuisance. A chewed cable in an attic is a fire risk.

In Stuart homes the common repair jobs involve attic insulation, duct wrap and flexible ducts, soffit and fascia edges, vent screens, and drywall around pipe penetrations. Wiring and plumbing are inspected and flagged but are repaired by the proper trade.

How we document damage.

The first step is a photo record. We photograph each area with damage and note its location, extent and likely cause. This record helps you plan repairs, brief an electrician or roofer and, where applicable, support an insurance claim. It also separates rodent damage from other problems, such as storm damage or age.

  • Soiled or compacted attic insulation.
  • Chewed or torn flexible duct and duct wrap.
  • Gnawed soffit, fascia and vent screens.
  • Holes in drywall near pipes and cabinets.
  • Chewed wiring insulation (flagged for an electrician).
  • Damaged weatherstripping and door seals.

Damage is priced as a separate line item, not buried in a larger number. You can accept some items and defer others.

What we repair.

We handle repairs that follow naturally from the rodent work. Insulation removal and replacement, patching gnawed soffit or vent screens, closing holes with metal and masonry, and repairing small sections of drywall. Roof and structural repairs, electrical work and plumbing are referred to the right tradesperson.

Duct damage deserves attention. A torn duct leaks cooled air into the attic and can circulate droppings and odor. In a climate where air conditioning runs most of the year, that costs money and comfort. We note any damaged duct and suggest repair or replacement.

Repair what the animals broke, and close what they used to get in.

Repair and sealing go together.

A repair without a seal invites more damage. Patching a chewed soffit with something soft just gives rodents a new target. We use durable materials, such as metal flashing, hardware cloth and cement patch, so the repair doubles as an entry seal. Our exclusion and entry point sealing page lists what we use.

Order matters. The animals are removed first, the entry points are sealed second, and the damage is repaired third. Otherwise a repair can trap an animal inside or fall victim to the next round.

Wiring, safety and when to wait.

Chewed wiring is the one item we ask you to leave alone. If the power is on and the insulation is gnawed, there is a shock and fire risk. Turn off the circuit if you can do so safely and call an electrician. We flag the location so they can find it.

Some damage can wait. Light cosmetic gnawing on wood trim, or a small chewed corner on a box, does not need urgent repair. We tell you which items are urgent and which are not, so you can budget.

Planning repairs in the right order.

  • Remove the animals first so new damage does not appear.
  • Seal the entry points so the repairs are not chewed again.
  • Clean and sanitize contaminated areas.
  • Repair or replace insulation, ducts and screens.
  • Bring in an electrician for any chewed wiring.
  • Take final photographs for your records.

Following this order keeps costs down. Doing it out of sequence is how people end up paying for the same repair twice.

Repairs after a storm or a roof job.

Rodent damage often sits beside other damage. A roof repair after a storm may have opened a gap that rodents used. A storm may also have wet an attic where rodents had already nested. Sorting out which damage came from which cause matters for claims and for repairs.

We note what looks like rodent damage and what looks like something else, and we say plainly when we cannot tell. That honesty helps you talk to your insurer and your contractor.

Older buildings and newer homes.

Older wood buildings, such as the historic structures around downtown Stuart, may have original materials that are hard to match. In those cases, we recommend repairs that are compatible and do not harm the original construction. Newer block homes have different weak points, usually at utility penetrations and soffits.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent damage repair

Do you fix wiring?

No. We flag chewed wiring and recommend a licensed electrician to repair it.

Will you give me a price for repairs separately?

Yes. Repairs are listed as their own items so you can choose which to do.

Is rodent damage covered by insurance?

Policies vary and often exclude it. Check your policy, and keep the photo record we provide.
