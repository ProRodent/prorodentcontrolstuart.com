Around the Lyric Theatre, 1926

The Lyric Theatre has stood in downtown Stuart since 1926 and is one of the best-known buildings in the district. The restaurants and older masonry around it are the setting for what this page describes: shared walls, rear service lanes and dumpsters. If you manage a building on these blocks, those are the first places we check. We mention the theatre as a landmark of the area, not as a customer.

The commercial and governmental heart of Stuart.

The Downtown Stuart Historic District is the historic commercial and civic core of the city, and the county seat of Martin County is built around it. The district was added to the National Register of Historic Places, as announced by the Florida Department of State. Its buildings come mostly from the early to mid twentieth century and include stores, offices, public buildings and some homes.

That mix matters for pest control. A downtown block is not a single structure. It is a row of buildings with different owners, different ages and different habits, joined by shared walls, alleys and service lanes. Rodents move through the joints between them, so a problem in one unit often reaches the next.

Where rodents travel downtown.

Rats in a historic downtown usually follow the service side of the block. They run along rear walls, under dumpster pads, through gaps beside back doors and along the utility lines that connect buildings. Roof rats take an overhead route along awnings, cables, parapets and any vegetation that reaches the roofline.

Inside, the older buildings hide a lot of space. Drop ceilings, old chases, boarded-up stairwells and gaps between a stucco front and the original wall make routes that are hard to see. A technician checks the rear and roof first, then follows the chases inward.

  • Rear doors with worn sweeps and gaps at the frame.
  • Dumpster pads, grease bins and flattened cardboard stacks.
  • Old pipe, cable and conduit openings in shared walls.
  • Awnings, parapets and rooflines touched by vegetation.
  • Storage rooms and apartments above ground-floor shops.
On a historic block, the neighbor's wall is part of your problem.

Restaurants, shops and the evening crowd.

Downtown has restaurants, cafes, shops and entertainment, with theatre traffic that peaks in the evening. Busy nights mean full bins and cardboard at the back door. If a dumpster lid stays open or a grease container overflows, rats notice within days.

Businesses can reduce pressure with simple habits. Keep dumpster lids closed, rinse floor drains, store food off the floor and flatten cardboard daily. Details on how monitoring and records work for a business are on our commercial rodent control page.

Sealing historic buildings carefully.

Old masonry and wood deserve a light touch. We do not drill, strip or cover original features without discussing it first. Where gaps need closing, we choose reversible or compatible materials, such as metal mesh and cementitious patches matched to the wall, rather than heavy foam that cannot be undone. Our exclusion and entry point sealing page lists the materials.

Building owners who plan repairs to a listed property should check local rules before major changes. A pest visit is not a restoration, and we flag openings so the right person can decide how to fix them.

Residents and upstairs tenants.

People who live above storefronts or in houses near the district also see activity. Noises in a ceiling shared with a restaurant, droppings in a kitchen over a food business, or a smell in a wall are common. Because an apartment cannot fix the building below, we explain what can be done from the unit and what the owner needs to address.

The river and the coast are close, so humidity is high and vegetation stays thick, which keeps both mice and rats active throughout the year. Our rat control and mouse control pages describe how the plan differs between the two.

A quick walk-through checklist for owners.

  • Rear door sweeps, frames and thresholds.
  • Dumpster lids, pads and grease bin seals.
  • Gaps where pipes and cables leave the building.
  • Roof access, parapets and awnings touched by branches.
  • Storage rooms with cardboard against walls.
  • Floor drains and the spaces under kitchen equipment.

Walk these on the same day each week. Owners who do this catch problems while they are still a few droppings and not a colony. If you find anything, photograph it and call.

Before we arrive

  • Keep pets leashed or indoors if the activity is in the yard or garage.
  • Do not spray household cleaners on droppings before the inspection.
  • Do not block a gap you found without telling us, since we want to see it first.
  • Jot down where you saw the animal and which direction it ran.
