A different county and a very different size.

Port Saint Lucie is an incorporated city in St. Lucie County, which is the neighboring county north of Martin County. It was incorporated in 1961 and had about 204,851 residents in the 2020 census, which makes it the sixth most populous city in Florida. It is far larger than Stuart, so we treat it differently from the smaller communities on our list.

Because the city is so big, only part of it is in range. Five of its ZIP codes sit within about 15 miles of Stuart. One more, 34987, lies roughly 16.4 miles away and is outside our confirmed coverage, so this page does not list it.

Jurisdiction is simple here. Every address is inside the city of Port Saint Lucie, which has its own government, and in St. Lucie County. That differs from Palm City, Jensen Beach and Hobe Sound, which are unincorporated and in Martin County.

What a large city means for a rodent visit.

A big city grew over decades, so building ages vary by area. Because much of Port Saint Lucie developed after its 1961 incorporation, many homes are likely newer than the oldest buildings in downtown Stuart. Newer homes have their own weak points, such as gaps at tile roof returns, unsealed line sets and soffit joints.

Large neighborhoods also have shared edges, such as canals, retention ponds and wooded lots, where animals travel between properties. Roof rats follow palms and hedges, and Norway rats follow ground cover. We ask what borders your lot, since that matters more than the street name.

  • Scratching in an attic or ceiling at night.
  • Droppings in a garage, pantry or laundry room.
  • Rats seen on fences, wires or rooflines near dusk.
  • A burrow beside a slab, shed or pool pad.
  • A smell with no obvious source.
In a city this size, the ZIP code decides the drive, and the lot decides the plan.

How we handle the work.

The plan is the same as everywhere we work. Rodent trapping and removal clears what is inside, and exclusion and entry point sealing closes the entry points. Inspection comes first, and you hear the price before work starts.

For homes with attics that hold air handlers, we add a look at duct wrap and insulation, which sometimes leads to attic and crawl space cleanup.

What to expect from the first visit.

The technician begins at the roofline and works inward through the attic and garage. In many newer homes, the weak points are at roof tile returns, soffits and utility penetrations, and we check those carefully. You hear what was found and what each step would cost before work begins.

Because the city is large, we also ask about the neighborhood setting. A lot beside a canal or a wooded edge has different pressure than one in the middle of a block, and the plan shifts accordingly.

A follow-up confirms the result. If activity stops, the job is done. If it continues, we widen the inspection to find the remaining gap.
