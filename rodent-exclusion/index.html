Exclusion is the fix that lasts.

Trapping removes the animals in the house today. Exclusion keeps the next ones out. Without it, a cleared attic can be repopulated within weeks, because the scent trails, food sources and open gaps that attracted the first group still exist. This is the part of the job that turns a treatment into a lasting result.

Public health guidance describes the same idea as seal up, trap up, clean up. The order of those steps is flexible, but none of them can be skipped if you want the problem to stay solved.

Where gaps hide on Stuart houses.

The common entries on block-and-stucco homes are small and easy to overlook. A gap around the line set where the air conditioner pipes enter the wall. A hole behind a hose bib. A bent soffit panel. Weep holes at the base of veneer walls. The ridge or end tile on a tile roof where mortar has cracked and fallen out.

Older wood buildings, of the kind that characterize the Downtown Stuart Historic District, have a different list. Foundation vents that have lost their screens, gaps where porches were added to original walls, and pipe chases cut decades ago and never sealed.

  • Tile returns, ridge caps and eave ends.
  • Gable and soffit vents without metal mesh.
  • Line set, conduit and plumbing penetrations.
  • Garage door corners and bottom seals.
  • Dryer vents, bath fans and range hoods.
  • Weep holes and foundation vents.
  • Gaps around pool equipment and utility pads.

Materials we use and why.

Steel wool is the folk remedy, and in humid air it rusts away within a season. We prefer stainless or galvanized hardware cloth, copper mesh, aluminum flashing and a masonry sealant or cementitious patch suited to each location. Foam alone is not a barrier, since rodents chew it.

Work at the roofline is done with care for the roof covering. We do not remove tiles beyond what is needed, and we flag any area where a roofer should assess underlying damage.

Timing around hurricane season.

Storms test every seal. High winds can lift tile edges, and flooding can flush burrows and push rodents indoors. Scheduling a sealing visit before the heart of hurricane season, which peaks in late summer and early fall, gives the work time to be checked and adjusted. After a storm, a quick inspection of the roofline and ground-level openings is worthwhile.

A seal that cannot be chewed, rusted or washed out is the only seal worth paying for.

What we ask before we seal.

Before closing anything, we confirm that no animals remain inside the space. We check for fresh droppings, listen at night where possible and use one-way devices on the main exits when the situation calls for them. Sealing a live colony in a wall is the single most common cause of the strong odor calls we take later.

We also ask what the homeowner wants preserved. A visible patch on a stucco wall, a cover plate on a front elevation and a screen on a decorative vent all affect how the house looks, and those choices are yours.

How long sealing lasts.

Well-done sealing holds up far longer than foam or steel wool. Stainless mesh, flashing and masonry patches resist rust, rot and washout in a way those fillers do not. The weak points over time are the sealant at the edges and any soft material used as a filler, so we avoid both where we can.

A yearly look at the roofline and the base of the walls catches any change. Storms, tree growth and new work on the house can open a gap that was closed, and a short check keeps the seal sound.

How we confirm it worked.

After sealing, the follow-up visit checks traps, droppings and the sealed locations. If new gnaw marks appear at a seal, we upgrade the material. If activity stops, the job is done. That is why we pair this service with rodent trapping and removal and a return visit.

You can help by trimming branches back from the roof, keeping hedges off the wall, storing firewood and debris away from the foundation and keeping garage doors closed after dark.

If the entry point is damage you cannot see, like chewed fascia or ducts, our rodent damage repair service covers fixing what the animals destroyed.

Questions about rodent exclusion and entry point sealing

How long does sealing take?

A typical house takes a few hours to a day depending on the number of openings and roof access. Larger or older properties take longer.

Why not just use caulk or foam?

Rodents chew soft materials. Metal, mesh and masonry hold up, which is why we use them where gaps are large enough to matter.

Will sealing trap animals inside?

We trap and clear the animals before sealing the main entries, or install one-way exits where appropriate, so nothing is shut inside.
